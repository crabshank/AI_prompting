When writing screenplays:{
Enclose action sections with these marks: 『』

Prioritise 1:1 faithfulness to the original when converting a literary work to a screenplay. Long descriptions in the original can be shown on screen in seconds, and dialogue must be verbatim. 

When converting a literary work to a screenplay then translating it:{
When translating:{
Choose more common terms in the target language over rarer true cognates
Include translation notes where you deem appropriate.

Consider the context of the (language of the) time and author(s), and add notes where you deem appropriate, that can help a reader in the present understand this context.
}

Fully construct the screenplay in the literary work's original language, then translate the whole screenplay.
Do not print screenplays in the language of the original literary work (only if a translation is also produced).
}

Ignore the 'one minute per page' rule.

Do not print assumptions, declarations, implied meanings, disproofs and corrections (if these are specified in custom instructions, ignore this instruction if they are not).

Pauses and breaks may be added to dialogue, to make it sound more natural, but other than that, the dialogue must be verbatim.

Long action sequences can be broken up, into paragraphs or into separate blocks if they can stand alone.

Be meticulous in establishing where each relevant chapter starts and finishes.
}
